# Dark Sapphire A11y

The accessibility build of [Dark Sapphire](notes.md). Same theme - same black, same card, same DM Sans,
same 40 `visualStyles`, same sapphire lead - with a palette that a colour-blind reader and a mono
printer can both take apart.

Generated by [`../_build-gem-themes.py`](../_build-gem-themes.py) (`--a11y`). Do not hand-edit.

## What it gives up, and why it has to

**An accessible version of a monochrome theme is not monochrome.** That is not a shortcut, it is
the whole problem: separation for a colour-blind reader has to come from somewhere, and in a
single-hue ramp there is nowhere for it to come from. Dark Sapphire measures **1.14:1** in greyscale
across its leading four and fails the CVD gate outright.

So slot 0 stays sapphire - it leads every chart, and with the metal ink and the black surface it is
where the theme's identity actually lives - and slots 1-3 are searched across the hue circle.

**The hues were searched, not named.** A greedy farthest-point pass followed
by coordinate ascent over the whole circle, scored on the worst CVD pair across
both deficiencies, with a 40-degree floor on plain hue so normal vision gets
its own separation. That last constraint was not in the first run, and it
showed: the search put a light gold and a dark gold in dark-gold's gated four
and two greens in dark-emerald's. Both passed every metric - the luminance gap
satisfies greyscale and CVD alike - and both read as one colour at two
brightnesses.

The constraint picks the colours and the names are applied afterwards. Hand-
picking a set and defending it is how you end up with crimson, gold and forest
in the same gated four, which collapses under both conditions at once.

## Semantics were replaced, not inherited

dark-ruby's `good` / `bad` do not survive a review:

- `bad` was `#CC0000` at **2.44:1** on the card - under the 3:1 non-text gate. Now `#DF4F39`
  at 3.63:1.
- `good` was `#6FBF73`, a true green. Red-versus-green is the exact pair both common
  deficiencies collapse. Now `#2DB59A` - a bluish-green, which is Okabe-Ito's answer: it keeps a
  red hue for normal vision while still separating under simulation. Measured **0.314**
  against `bad`.
- `tableAccent` was `#990000` at 1.61:1. Now `#357FE2` at 3.61:1, on the lead's hue.

## What is still not gated

Gridlines stay low-contrast (2.16:1). WCAG's non-text rule covers meaningful graphics, and
a gridline is scaffolding - lifting it to 3:1 makes every chart noisier for every reader, which is
a worse outcome than leaving it quiet. If you need heavier gridlines for a specific audience, that
is a per-report override rather than a theme change.

Text still sits at 5.68:1 or better against the card, inherited unchanged from dark-ruby.

## Apply it

```bash
pbir theme serialize dark-sapphire-a11y-v1.0.json -o /tmp/dark-sapphire-a11y.Theme
pbir theme build /tmp/dark-sapphire-a11y.Theme -o "<project>.Report" -f --clean
```
